# Stage 1C.2.6 — Myanmar overlap batch 01: evidence review v001

**Status:** evidence-based recommendation only; no reviewer disposition is approved.  
**Input batch:** `STAGE1C2_MYANMAR_BATCH_01_V001`  
**Scope:** the seven frozen target-catalogue events in the geometric intersection of the original Himalaya–Tibet and Sunda–Indonesia exploratory rectangles.  
**Prepared:** 2026-11-09.

## 1. Executive finding

The seven events should not be treated as one homogeneous seismogenic source population merely because they fall inside the intersection of two exploratory rectangles and inside the broad PB2002 `Persia-Tibet-Burma` orogen polygon.

The event-specific evidence resolves into three defensible *candidate* tectonic groups:

1. **Shan-Thai distributed deformation:** 2011 Tachileik event (`usp000hyfx`).
2. **Sagaing Fault system:** 2012 Shwebo event (`usp000jvbq`) and the two 28 March 2025 Mandalay events (`us7000pn9s`, `us7000pn9z`).
3. **Indo-Burmese wedge / Indian lithosphere:** 2016 Manipur (`us10004b2n`), Kani (`us20005hqz`), and Chauk (`us10006gbf`), with the latter two supported as intraslab events by event-specific or paired-event literature.

These groups may remain under a broad Burma–Shan-Thai regional umbrella for geographic inventory, but should be represented as distinct child populations for any source-type or sequence modelling. Do not collapse them into one homogeneous magnitude-completeness or sequence population without a scientific rationale.

## 3. Source-specific detail and limitations

### 3.1 `usp000hyfx` — Tachileik, 24 March 2011

USGS describes the earthquake as occurring in a region of distributed deformation in the Shan-Thai block. Its event-specific tectonic summary notes northeast-trending, left-lateral faults in the region and says the focal mechanism is compatible with potential left-lateral slip on a NE-trending nodal plane. The USGS summary locates the Sagaing Fault about 400 km west of this earthquake. This is strong evidence for the Shan-Thai candidate domain and against treating this event as a Sagaing Fault event. It does not uniquely identify a causative local fault. Sources R01 and R17.

### 3.2 `usp000jvbq` — Shwebo, 11 November 2012

An EGU General Assembly abstract describes right-lateral motion on the Sagaing Fault and a preliminary 60–70 km rupture length. The Myanmar Earthquake Committee also linked the event to Sagaing Fault activity. A later World Bank GRADE report summarizes the 2012 earthquake as rupturing approximately 45 km of the Sagaing segment between Singu and Sabeanago. For regional population construction, this event should be separated from the Shan-Thai distributed-deformation group. The differing rupture-length values are estimates from different sources and should not be averaged or treated as interchangeable. Capture the reviewed event-specific moment-tensor/fault-plane product before a final source-mechanism label is frozen. Sources R02–R04, R16 and R17.

### 3.3 `us10004b2n` — Manipur / Imphal, 3 January 2016

The USGS event-specific tectonic summary describes strike-slip faulting within the complex India–Eurasia plate-boundary region, states that the event occurred close to 50 km depth within the India-plate lithosphere, and explicitly says the causative fault is unknown. Gahalaut and Kundu (2016) interpret the event as an intra-slab earthquake in the Indo-Burmese wedge and discuss its focal mechanism. These descriptions are compatible with a broad Indo-Burmese/India-lithosphere candidate domain, but they do not justify assigning the event to a named fault. Keep a boundary/junction hold for the exact source classification. Sources R05–R06 and R17.

### 3.4 `us20005hqz` — Kani / Mawlaik, 13 April 2016

Aung et al. (2019) explicitly identify the Mw 6.9 Kani earthquake as an intra-slab event. The reviewed USGS W-phase moment tensor has a centroid depth of 140.5 km and 97% double-couple, with reverse/oblique nodal-plane rakes. The frozen catalogue hypocentral depth is 136 km. These are two distinct depth parameters, not a conflict to be silently resolved. The intraslab child-domain recommendation is strong, while a formal slab-relative coordinate should still be derived from a named slab model before any exact slab position is reported. Sources R07–R08 and R17.

### 3.5 `us10006gbf` — Chauk, 24 August 2016

Shiddiqi et al. (2018) provide an event-specific source study describing an intermediate-depth intraslab earthquake, with a thrust focal mechanism and source-depth analysis around 90 km. Aung et al. (2019) also treat Chauk as one of the two 2016 Myanmar intraslab events. The frozen catalogue's preferred hypocentral depth is 82 km. Preserve 82 km as the target-catalogue value and record the separate source-study/model depth with its provenance. Sources R07, R09 and R17.

### 3.6 `us7000pn9s` — Mandalay, 28 March 2025

USGS lists the preferred W-phase moment tensor as strike-slip; its preferred finite-fault product models a multi-segment rupture. Goldberg et al. (2025) report an approximately 475 km supershear rupture of the Sagaing Fault. A USGS publication dated 21 April 2026 likewise describes shallow dextral faulting and a 475 km surface rupture. The event belongs in a distinct Sagaing Fault candidate domain. Keep the catalogue hypocentral depth (10 km) separate from the W-phase centroid depth (40.5 km). Sources R10–R12 and R17.

### 3.7 `us7000pn9z` — Mw 6.7 event, 28 March 2025

USGS ComCat lists this event at 06:32:04 UTC, 10 km depth and Mww 6.7. The BSSA preliminary rupture analysis describes an Mw 6.7 aftershock about 11 minutes after the mainshock and reports a GCMT centroid depth of 19.1 km and dip of 73 degrees. The World Bank GRADE report separately notes a notable Mw 6.7 strike-slip aftershock about 12 minutes after the mainshock; its report does not provide the ComCat ID, but the time and magnitude align with this event's ComCat origin. A June 2026 aftershock study finds that aftershock spatial distribution is controlled by the north–south Sagaing Fault and related to mainshock slip, fault geometry and historical rupture zones.

**Location discrepancy requiring retention:** a geodesic calculation from the frozen catalogue coordinates (mainshock 22.0110°N, 95.9363°E; M6.7 event 21.6975°N, 95.9690°E) gives 35.023 km. The BSSA abstract/text reports approximately 59 km south. These values likely reflect different origin/location solutions or reference positions; the evidence reviewed here does not resolve the difference. Do not choose one value silently.

The event may be retained in the Sagaing Fault child population on the strength of the published aftershock interpretation, but this regional review does not assign a sequence role, merge the two ComCat IDs, or approve a mainshock/aftershock label in the target data. Sources R04, R13–R15 and R17.

## 4. Recommended handling for the seven-event overlap

- Retain all seven events in the frozen target catalogue.
- Retain the broad Burma–Shan-Thai complex as an umbrella geography if useful for inventory.
- Do not treat the seven events as a single homogeneous source population.
- Represent the three candidate child groups separately: Shan-Thai distributed deformation; Sagaing Fault; Indo-Burmese wedge / Indian lithosphere.
- Distinguish event hypocentral depth from moment-tensor centroid depth and source-study depth.
- Do not infer a causative fault solely from Hasterok subplate names, PB2002 orogen membership, nearest-boundary distance/type, or USGS place strings.
- Keep event sequence roles out of this regional adjudication. The 2025 M6.7 has published aftershock evidence, but sequence modelling and event-role assignment belong in the later sequence-reconstruction stage.
