# LAIC-GUARD Project Charter

## Purpose

Test, rather than assume, whether independent observations from the lithosphere, lower atmosphere, ionosphere and electromagnetic environment exhibit statistically unusual, spatially coherent and temporally coordinated behaviour before selected earthquakes.

The predictive question is stricter: does this information improve out-of-sample earthquake forecasts beyond conventional seismicity-only and environmental baselines?

## Primary principle

An anomaly is not automatically an earthquake precursor. A precursor claim requires:
1. A defined and reproducible observation.
2. Appropriate environmental and space-weather controls.
3. Comparison with matched non-event periods and regions.
4. Evidence of spatial/temporal consistency.
5. Independent event-level validation.
6. Incremental forecast skill over credible baselines.

## Research domains

- Lithosphere/seismicity: event rate, b-value, fractal dimension, GNSS deformation, InSAR, groundwater/geochemistry where available.
- Atmosphere: LST, OLR, aerosols, trace gases, temperature, humidity, pressure, precipitation, wind and cloud.
- Ionosphere: TEC/VTEC, dTEC/dt, ROTI, foF2, hmF2, B0, B1, foEs/h'Es and electron-density profiles.
- Electromagnetic: ULF spectral power, Z/H ratio, polarization, cross-station coherence and spectral features.
- Confounders: solar/geomagnetic indices, solar wind, meteorology, local time, season, instrument state, QA and human noise.

## What success means

Success is not necessarily a positive precursor result. A high-quality result may show that one or more domains do not add forecast skill after controls. The project succeeds scientifically if it provides a transparent, reproducible and well-calibrated test.

## Scope boundary

No operational earthquake warning, public alarm, or claim of deterministic prediction is in scope. Any future probability output is an experimental research forecast and must be labelled as such.
